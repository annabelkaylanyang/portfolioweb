CONTEXT

Zrø is a space for thinking through everyday uncertainty.

Zrø is designed for the small uncertainties people carry through everyday life. Whether it's a decision they’re unsure about or an interaction they keep playing, Zrø reflects users’ own thoughts back from a different angle instead of providing direct advice. The premise is that the most useful shift often isn’t deciding what to do, but changing how the situation is understood in the first place.

ROLE

I led the redesign of Zrø’s product experience and interface.

I turned user-testing findings into new interaction models, like replacing the chat interface with a continuous thought stream. I also built Zrø’s identity system, from logo to motion, across 20+ screens and components.

PROBLEM

Users looked to Zrø for answers instead of reflecting on their own thoughts.

In early testing, 5 of 6 participants repeatedly treated Zrø as a conversational partner. They asked for advice, waited for the next response, and continued directing questions toward the AI even after it became clear that Zrø would not provide direct solutions.

Responses weren’t the issue: when participants did stop to interpret a response, some reconsidered what they’d originally written. We suspected the interface instead. Alternating message bubbles, distinct AI colors and a familiar back-and-forth rhythm framed every response as the next turn in a conversation. I led the redesign that tested our hypothesis.

GOAL

How might the interface shift attention from the AI’s response back to the user’s own thinking?

Success meant reversing what we saw in testing. I used this question to evaluate whether each design decision moved users toward three reflection behaviors: pause, reconsider and continue:

Since interaction patterns shape how users understand a product before any explanation does, the interface became the first place to intervene.

BREAK THE MESSAGE-BY-MESSAGE MODEL

How can reflection stop feeling like a series of messages?

Early versions of Zrø moved from chat bubbles to cards, but both still treated reflection as a sequence of separate exchanges. I wanted to rethink the interaction itself, not just how each message looked.

FIRST DIRECTION

I removed the two-speaker layout, but separating exchanges separated the thought.

I explored a horizontal swipe model that paired the user’s thought and Zrø’s response on one screen. Without the left/right layout, it no longer felt like two speakers, and swiping back to earlier moments felt familiar.

In walkthroughs with the founder, swiping helped Pause: each moment got full attention. But it weakened Reconsider. Earlier thoughts were never visible next to the current one, so it was hard to see how ideas developed.

Learning

Removing the chat structure wasn’t enough. Reflection also needed continuity.

FINAL DIRECTION

I reframed the exchange as one continuous train of thought.

Inspired by how Spotify’s lyrics view keeps surrounding lines visible while bringing one into focus, I designed reflections as a continuous stream. This was designed to support Reconsider by keeping earlier thoughts visible, and Pause by allowing one exchange to fill the screen again.

Zrø’s Chat UI

Users could scroll through the larger stream, then tap an exchange to bring it to focus.

TRADEOFF

More continuity, but less authorship.

In one stream, it got harder to tell whose words were whose, especially when looking back.

SOLUTION

Let type, not position, separate the user from Zrø.

I made the user’s words larger and bolder, and quieted Zrø’s response. The core tension was that Zrø needed to respond without becoming the center. Now it still responds, but the user’s own thinking is the most prominent thing on screen, and what they build from next.

That turned to a new question, How should users return to a reflection that isn’t a chat log?

MAKING REFLECTIONS
REVISITABLE

How should users return to a reflection that isn’t a chat log?

Most AI tools organize history for retrieval: titles, dates, and a sidebar make past tasks easy to find. But Zrø wasn’t designed around retrieving information. Returning should bring back the state of mind behind a reflection.

MAKING REFLECTIONS
REVISITABLE

I built each memory around its turning point, not its title

Each session became a visual space previewed by one significant exchange. The system algorithmically selects an exchange that may represent a turning point in the user’s thinking. The user’s own words remain visually dominant, carrying the same hierarchy from the live reflection into memory.

TRADEOFF

More meaning, less scannability.

In design review, we realized the visual memory system gave each reflection more context, but sacrificed some of the quick scanning people expect from conventional history.

SOLUTION

Bring titles back, in sync.

I reintroduced a lightweight title list for fast retrieval. Selecting a title immediately brings its visual memory into focus, so finding a known reflection and rediscovering one can coexist. Knowing which familiar convention to keep mattered as much as knowing which to remove.

A more reflective model was also a less familiar one.

Across the stream and memory system, I had replaced conventions users already knew from chat. That raised the next question:

How should users enter an experience that no longer behaves like chat?

SOLUTION

The first interaction had to teach users how to approach Zrø

By replacing familiar chat patterns across both reflection and memory, Zrø became more aligned with its purpose — but also less immediately recognizable. The homepage became the first opportunity to set expectations before users entered the experience.

DIRECTION

Turn the product philosophy into an interaction

Rather than explaining reflection through instructions, I used gradual focus as the interaction itself. Inspired by aperture, I explored blur, motion, and shifting clarity to make the homepage move from uncertainty toward focus as users engage with it.

TAKEAWAYS

Onboarding didn’t have to mean more explanation. The interaction itself could demonstrate how Zrø asks users to slow down, engage, and let clarity emerge.

Reflections

What I enjoyed most was realizing how much meaning can live in seemingly small interface decisions. A bubble, a swipe, or a line of type can quietly tell someone whether they’re supposed to talk, browse, pause, or think. Zrø made me much more curious about designing those invisible cues deliberately.

If I continued on Zrø’s interface, I would validate the final system more directly against Pause, Reconsider, and Continue. A lot of my decisions were shaped through design review and qualitative feedback, and I’d want to see more directly how those patterns changed the way people actually thought and responded over time.

Prior to this project, I had limited experience with WebGL. It was fun to explore a new tool, and I’m excited to work more with experimental visuals in the future. Super grateful for the opportunity to expand into visuals and product launch toward the end of my project!
